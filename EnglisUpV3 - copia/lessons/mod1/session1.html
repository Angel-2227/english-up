IPA & Speaking — Sounds of English 🔤
⏱ 15 min
🔤 The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) shows you exactly how to pronounce any word in English.
Why IPA? English spelling is unpredictable. "brave" /breɪv/, "kind" /kaɪnd/, "young" /jʌŋ/ — the letters don't always match the sounds. IPA gives you a universal code that always does.

Adjective Order — The Right Sequence 📐
⏱ 12 min
🤔 Why does "a brave young man" sound right but "a young brave man" sounds strange?
In English, when you use more than one adjective before a noun, they follow a fixed order. Native speakers feel it instinctively — and now you will too.

For now, we focus on the first 3 layers. The rest (color, origin, material) comes in Module 5.
1st
OPINION
brave, kind, funny, selfless, reckless
→
2nd

🎬 Examples from the film
Manolo is a brave [opinion] young [age] man. ✅ Joaquín is a strong [opinion] tall [size] young [age] soldier. ✅ ❌ "a young brave man" — wrong! Opinion always comes before age.
💡 Memory trick: "OSA" — Opinion → Size → Age.
Opinion comes first because it's your view. Size and age are objective facts.

Grammar — Verb To Be ⭐
⏱ 18 min
Verb To Be is the most important verb in English. In Spanish it works like ser and estar together — but in English it's just one verb.

We use it to say who someone is, what they are like, and where they are.
🎬 Verb To Be — in the film
"He is a musician." → Manolo is a musician (not a soldier). "They are friends." → Manolo and Joaquín are friends. "She is not afraid." → María is not (isn't) afraid of anything. "Is he brave?" → Yes, he is! / No, he isn't.
📌 Full table — Present & Past
Subject🟢 Present🔵 Past
I am — I am brave. was — I was afraid.
He / She / It is — She is kind. was — He was young.
You / We / They are — They are funny. were — They were friends.
📌 Positive · Negative · Question
Subject✅ Positive❌ Negative❓ Question
I I am Manolo. I am not a soldier.
(I'm not…)		 Am I right?
He / She She is brave. He is not reckless.
(He isn't…)		 Is she brave?
They / We They are friends. We are not enemies.
(We aren't…)		 Are they friends?
Past – I/He/She He was young. She was not alone.
(She wasn't…)		 Was he alone?
💡 Quick rules:
1. For questions, move Verb To Be before the subject: "She is kind." → "Is she kind?"
2. For negatives, add not: "They are friends." → "They are not friends." (= aren't)
3. Short answers: "Is she brave?" → "Yes, she is." / "No, she isn't." (never "Yes, she is brave.")
